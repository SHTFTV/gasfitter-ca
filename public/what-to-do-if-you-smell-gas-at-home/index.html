What to Do If You Smell Gas at Home in BC

November 24, 2026

If you smell gas at home, stop what you're doing, get everyone outside, and call FortisBC's 24-hour emergency line at 1-800-663-9911 or dial 911 once you're out. That's FortisBC's own advice: leave first, call second, and stay outside until someone tells you it's safe to go back in. Don't stop to search for the source, and don't email or message FortisBC on social media about a possible leak; the utility says those channels aren't for emergencies. Call even if you're not sure it's gas. FortisBC says it would rather check than have you wait.

Winter is when furnaces, water heaters and fireplaces run hardest, so it's a good time to make sure everyone in the house knows these steps.

Why gas smells like rotten eggs

Natural gas has no smell of its own. FortisBC adds a small amount of a chemical called mercaptan so a leak is easy to notice. The utility describes the smell as rotten eggs or sulphur. You might also hear a hissing sound near an appliance or a gas pipe.

FortisBC also notes that natural gas is lighter than air, so it tends to rise and collect near ceilings, upper floors or in enclosed spaces.

The three steps, in order

1. Stop

Stop what you're doing. Leave your phone and electrical switches alone until you are safely outside. Avoid flames, smoking, or anything else that could create a spark.

2. Go outside

FortisBC's instructions say to leave the door open behind you as you exit, along with any windows that are already open. Get people and pets out first. Don't go back in for belongings.

3. Call from outside

Once you're outside and away from the house, call the FortisBC Emergency Line at 1-800-663-9911 (24 hours) or 911. The BC government's natural gas safety brochure adds the key last step: stay outside until an official says it's safe to go back in.

What happens after you call

A FortisBC technician comes to investigate. According to FortisBC, there's no charge for a technician to attend and investigate a gas smell inside your home. The utility's emergency staff handle calls about gas odours, line damage and carbon monoxide.

What happens next depends on what they find:

Where the problem is Who typically fixes it Notes
FortisBC's meter or service line FortisBC FortisBC says meters and service lines belong to it, so there's no cost to customers for repairs, unless you damaged the line while digging
Piping after the meter, inside or outside the house A licensed gas contractor you hire The appliance or line may be shut off until it's repaired
A gas appliance (furnace, water heater, range, fireplace) A licensed gas contractor you hire Get it repaired before using it again
Nothing found No repair needed You still did the right thing by calling

FortisBC keeps an online directory of licensed natural gas contractors if you need someone after the visit.

What not to do

  • Don't try to find or fix the leak yourself.
  • Don't stay inside to make the call.
  • Don't assume the smell is coming from the garbage or the drain. FortisBC says many callers hesitate because they aren't sure, and it wants the call anyway.
  • Don't rely on email, an online form or social media. These aren't monitored as emergency channels.
  • Don't go back inside until an official says it's safe.

Smelling gas outside

If you notice the smell near the meter, in the yard or on the street, the same rules apply: move away and call the emergency line or 911. FortisBC says it will send someone to check whether the smell is inside or outside. If you've hit a gas line while digging, leave it alone and call; don't try to cover or bury it.

Gas smell vs carbon monoxide

These are different hazards. A gas leak usually has that rotten-egg smell. Carbon monoxide, which can come from a malfunctioning or poorly vented appliance, is a gas FortisBC describes as one you can't see or smell. That's why a working CO alarm matters even in a home where nobody has ever smelled gas.

If your sense of smell isn't reliable

Some people have a weakened sense of smell from age, illness or medication. FortisBC points out that a natural gas detector works much like a smoke alarm, continuously checking the air and sounding if it detects gas. It can be worth adding one near gas appliances.

Lowering the odds of a leak

  • Keep up with appliance maintenance. FortisBC recommends regular servicing of gas appliances by a licensed contractor.
  • Click or call before you dig, so you know where buried gas lines run.
  • Keep the area around your gas meter clear, including of snow and ice this winter.
  • Teach everyone in the house, including kids and guests, the three steps.

If you live in a strata or manage a multi-unit building, a building-wide response plan helps everyone act the same way. The property-focused guides at Gasfitters.ca cover that side.

Make the plan before you need it

A few minutes now saves confusion later. Save 1-800-663-9911 in every phone in the house, and talk through where everyone would meet outside. Make sure older kids know they can call 911 themselves. If you rent out a suite, share the steps with your tenants too, since they may notice a smell before you do. After an earthquake, FortisBC gives the same instruction: if you smell rotten eggs or hear gas escaping, go outside right away and call.

The bottom line

Stop, go outside, and call FortisBC at 1-800-663-9911 or 911 from a safe distance. Stay out until you're told it's safe. Calling about a smell that turns out to be nothing costs you a little time; waiting on a real leak can cost far more.

Frequently asked questions

What number do I call if I smell gas in BC?

FortisBC's 24-hour emergency line is 1-800-663-9911, or you can dial 911. Call from outside, after everyone has left the house.

Does FortisBC charge to check a gas smell?

FortisBC says there's no charge for a technician to attend and investigate a gas smell inside your home. Repairs to your own piping or appliances are done by a licensed contractor you hire.

Should I open windows if I smell gas?

FortisBC's advice is to get outside and leave the door open behind you, along with any windows that are already open. Don't stay inside to open more.

What if I'm not sure it's gas?

Call anyway. FortisBC says it wants you to call even if you're unsure, and it will investigate whether the smell is inside or outside.
